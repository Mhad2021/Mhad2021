## LinkedIn

### LI-1 — the test they can run themselves

Ask ChatGPT who the best [your trade] in your city is.

It will name three. Maybe four.

Not a list of ten. Not a map with pins. Three names, stated as fact, to
someone who just asked a buying question.

If you are not one of them, you are not fourth. There is no fourth. The
answer simply does not contain you.

Thirty seconds to check on your own business. Most owners have never done it.

---

### LI-2 — the problem with no symptoms

Every marketing problem you have ever had announced itself.

Ad spend climbs, leads do not. Traffic dips. The phone goes quiet. A number
moves, and you go looking for why.

Here is the one that does not.

When someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation in your category and
it returns three companies that are not you, nothing happens on your end. No
impression. No bounce. No line in any report you own. The conversation never
touched your business, so your business holds no record of it.

You are not losing to a competitor who outranked you. You are losing to an
answer you never saw.

It is the only problem I know of in this business with no symptoms.

---

### LI-3 — the uncomfortable one about websites

Rebuilding your website does almost nothing for whether an AI recommends you.

That is an uncomfortable sentence if you have recently paid for one.

The reason is mechanical. These systems largely do not learn about your
company from your company. They learn from what other people wrote about
you: directories, reviews, forum threads, a trade publication, a customer
mentioning you somewhere you have never visited.

Your own site is one voice claiming you are good. That is the least
persuasive form of evidence there is, and it is the one most owners spend
their budget on.

Your homepage is the part of your reputation you fully control. It is also
the part that counts least.

---

### LI-4 — your competitor as the source of truth

Somewhere right now, an AI is explaining your industry to someone who is
about to spend money.

It learned how to do that from whoever wrote the clearest material about it.

If that was your competitor, then your competitor is currently the source of
truth about your category, and sometimes about you. Their framing. Their
definitions. Their comparison table, with their strengths as the criteria.

You do not get to reply. You are not in the room. You are a row in someone
else's table, if you are in it at all.

The business that explains a category tends to end up owning it. That used
to take a decade and a magazine.

---

### LI-5 — the scene

Three quotes on the table. The customer had already chosen before any of you
spoke.

Not from an advert. Not from a search results page. They asked an assistant
which companies to consider, got three names, and rang those three.

Your price was competitive. Your reviews were good. You were never in the
shortlist, because the shortlist was built before you had any chance to be
in it.

The sale did not go wrong. It went somewhere you could not see.
